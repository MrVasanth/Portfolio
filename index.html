How I think

I don't optimize
campaigns. I optimize
buying journeys.

Tools change. Channels change. Algorithms change. The thing that stays constant is the buyer trying to decide if you're worth a meeting. Everything I do is engineered around that decision.

01

High intent beats high traffic.

I'd rather spend $80K on 4,000 qualified clicks than $40K on 40,000 cheap ones. Volume is a vanity metric. Intent is a leading indicator of pipeline.

02

Most CAC problems are landing page problems.

Most teams scale spend when leads drop. I fix the page first. A mismatched headline, a seven-field form or a feature-first layout kills conversion before the bid strategy gets a chance. Spend scales after the page earns it.

03

Good reporting changes the next decision.

A dashboard nobody acts on is just decoration. I build reporting around payback, pipeline influenced and spend efficiency. Numbers that earn a budget conversation and tell Sales exactly where to focus.

04

The best ideas are already in the room.

Buyers tell you what they need through their search terms. Sales tells you what converts through their call notes. When Qualtrics announced sunsetting Delighted, I saw a ready-made audience before anyone briefed me on it. The best campaigns don't need a clever idea. They need someone paying attention.

05

Systems scale better than hacks.

One brilliant campaign is a story. A modular landing page system, a documented experiment cadence and a closed-loop reporting stack scales without you in the room. The mini-website, the CRO framework, the ABM sequence. All built to run, not to impress.

Case 01 · CRO · Landing page architecture

Fixing the page before touching the budget.

SurveySparrow's paid traffic was landing on standalone pages. A visitor who wanted to learn more had two options: fill the form or hit the back button. Scaling spend would only have bought more of the same exits.

I worked with Design and Front-end to build 25 no-indexed pages across 12 designs in two months. I then linked them into a mini-website with its own header and footer, so buyers could explore the product without leaving the paid flow. I also rebuilt the Qualtrics comparison page and added a demo-confirmed page with a 14-day trial, so booked prospects could try the product before the call. The same approach carried over to SparrowDesk, where the core page converts at 10.7% and the Zendesk comparison page at 12.5%.

Case 02 · ABM · Built from zero

Building ABM from zero, for two very different products.

SurveySparrow had never run ABM. I set it up for SparrowDesk, a new helpdesk product with no brand recognition, and for SurveySparrow, a 10-year-old product with $7M+ ARR. For SparrowDesk, I built a list of 16,000+ accounts already using competitor tools, using Factors.ai, Fibbler and Clay. LinkedIn ads introduced the product to them, competitor by competitor. The 15% who engaged moved to influencer thought-leadership ads, which reached a 12.5% engagement rate. Cold emails through Brevo then went to the same accounts, so the email was the second time they heard from us, not the first.

The results matched each product's stage. For SurveySparrow, $22K of spend influenced $438K in pipeline and $146K in revenue within 90 days. For SparrowDesk, $13K reached 70+ companies and opened 6 conversations, 2 of them positive. That's early, but it's expected for a product buyers are still discovering.

Case 03 · Regional paid strategy · EU

Growing EU pipeline on a smaller budget.

When I took over Europe, the campaigns were structured as one market, even though buyers in different countries search, compare and buy differently. Sales also had no clear view of what paid was bringing in, so every budget conversation started from zero.

I rebuilt the campaign structure with region-wise targeting and put spend behind the themes that were actually producing demos. I also built a HubSpot dashboard for the EU Sales Manager, so both teams looked at the same pipeline numbers. In H1 2024, EU pipeline grew from $415K to $530K while spend went down. By H2, payback held at 7 months, with a 2.2× spend-to-pipeline ratio and cost per demo under $1K.

Case 04 · Search demand · Sales-led

$186K in revenue from keywords Sales heard first.

Sales calls kept raising the same question: does SurveySparrow work with the tools we already use? Buyers were searching for surveys inside Salesforce, Zendesk and HubSpot, and we weren't showing up for any of it.

I went through customer conversations with the Sales team, built keyword groups around each integration, and gave each one its own landing page. I later turned the HubSpot page into a dynamic template, so new integration pages could go live without a fresh build.

Case 05 · Competitive timing

Turning a competitor's shutdown into pipeline in a week.

When Qualtrics announced it was sunsetting Delighted, every Delighted customer suddenly needed a replacement. The ones who searched first would settle on whoever showed up first.

Within a week, I had a Google Search campaign live targeting Delighted users, with a dedicated landing page offering free migration to SurveySparrow. The offer removed the biggest reason to wait, which was the effort of moving their surveys and data across.
